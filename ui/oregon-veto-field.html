Executive Summary

Analyst Synthesis

Oregon's public legislative data service carries a yes-or-no field, Vetoed, on every bill. From 2007 until last week it was false on 26 of the 27 measures governors vetoed, and only one bill in the service was marked as vetoed. We reported the gap on 28 September. By the next day the field was true on all 27, and the Legislature has since confirmed both the numbers and the correction.

  • On 27 September the Vetoed field disagreed with the bills' own action histories on 26 of 27 vetoed measures, across 9 of the 10 sessions that had vetoes, including all seven vetoes of 2025.
  • The field was corrected on all 27 by 29 September, 03:28 UTC, and still matched on 6 October.
  • The Legislature's reply reproduced the numbers and confirmed the update. It does not say the update was made because of the report.

SIG-001 Oregon's veto field contradicted the bills' own records on 26 of 27 vetoes Corroborated
On 27 September, an automated check compared each measure's Vetoed field with its action history across every session in the Legislature's data service. Twenty-seven measures from 2007 to 2026 record "Governor vetoed." Twenty-six read Vetoed = false. Twenty-three of those are vetoes that stood. Three (SB 545, SB 897 and SB 5520, all 2009) became law by override, so false was defensible for them. Only SB 215 (2013) was marked true.

Signal Assessment

Interpretation · Bounded

SIG-001 and SIG-003 together establish, with high confidence, that a structured field in Oregon's official legislative data disagreed with the Legislature's own history on nearly every veto since 2007. Anyone who asked the service which bills the governor vetoed got one bill back, not 24. The history rows were right the whole time. The field built to summarize them was not.

The baseline matters for scale. The major legislative-data vendors read veto status from the action history, so their Oregon data was already correct. The error reached people who trusted the field directly: small newsrooms, researchers and anyone building on the raw service. Comparable checks of Washington (2021–2026) and California (2025–26) found no equivalent defect.

SIG-002 shows the old state is gone. The live service now shows only the corrected values. The 27 September retrieval was bound into a hash chain the day it was made, and on 1 October it was sealed, with the corrected records, under the publisher's key. The pack shows that our records have not been edited since. It cannot show, independently of us, what the service returned; the Legislature's own reproduction of the 27 measures is the independent check.

What remains unknown: why the field was wrong for so long, what it was meant to represent, and whether the correction was made because of the report. With the overrides now marked true, Vetoed = true no longer means "did not become law."

What This Does Not Prove

Standing Limits
Publication Rule — A Pattern Is Not Proof of Intent
A wrong field is not evidence of concealment; the history rows recorded every veto correctly throughout. The timing of the correction does not prove it was made because of the report, and the Legislature's reply does not say so. No one was shown to have relied on the field to their harm. The disagreement is between a data field and the record it summarizes, and this check reports disagreement, not error.
